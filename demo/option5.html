The 1970s marked a transformative era in the American workplace, particularly for women in the media. Spurred by the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the subsequent rise of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC), women across the United States began to challenge entrenched patterns of sex discrimination in employment.

This digital archive explores the explosion of lawsuits filed under the EEOC against news organizations during the 1970s, focusing on the legal and social impact these cases had on the status of women in the workplace, media, and public life.

Historical Context

Between 1970 and the early 1980s, women at nearly every major print news organization in the United States filed class-action sex discrimination lawsuits. These cases exposed systemic patterns of gender discrimination, challenged rigid job hierarchies, and forced industry-wide reforms in hiring, promotion, and pay practices.

The lawsuits were driven by courageous women who risked their careers and visionary attorneys who established legal precedents that extended far beyond the media industry.

Why Choose "Courtroom"?

This design borrows from the visual language of the law — structured grids, numbered sections, a dark navy palette, and clean sans-serif typography that echoes federal court documents and institutional reports. A statistics bar immediately communicates scope and seriousness, while the timeline element provides at-a-glance orientation.

Personality: Authoritative, precise, and institutional. The design says "this is a matter of public record" and invites the visitor to examine the evidence. It conveys credibility without coldness, using subtle touches of lighter blue to soften the formality.

Reminds people of: Federal court system websites (redesigned), Brennan Center for Justice, the Equal Justice Initiative memorial site, or government data dashboards.

Best for audiences who prioritise data, structure, and institutional authority — researchers, legal scholars, journalists, and policy professionals. The design makes a strong case that this project is rigorous, well-organised, and built to be a definitive resource.
