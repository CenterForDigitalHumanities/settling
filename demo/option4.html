Settling: Women Who
Sued the News

Their voices changed history. This archive preserves the stories of women who demanded equality in America's newsrooms.

In the 1970s, women across the United States began to challenge entrenched patterns of sex discrimination — nowhere more visibly than in the nation's newsrooms.

Spurred by the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the subsequent rise of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC), they filed lawsuits that would transform American media. This digital archive explores those cases and the women behind them, serving as a companion to the forthcoming book by Ashley Walter, PhD.

"The lawsuits were driven by courageous women who risked their careers and visionary attorneys who established legal precedents."

Between 1970 and the early 1980s, women at nearly every major print news organization filed class-action sex discrimination lawsuits — exposing systemic patterns, challenging rigid hierarchies, and forcing industry-wide reforms.

Historical Context

These cases exposed systemic patterns of gender discrimination, challenged rigid job hierarchies, and forced industry-wide reforms in hiring, promotion, and pay practices.

The resulting consent decrees mandated hiring goals, affirmative action plans, pay equity measures, and back pay settlements — transforming not only the newsrooms where they were filed but the broader landscape of American media.

Why Choose "Voices"?

This design centres the human experience. Soft gradients in muted purple and warm earth tones create a space that feels intimate and personal — like a quiet conversation rather than a lecture. Rounded cards and generous whitespace invite exploration without overwhelming.

Personality: Warm, empathetic, and quietly powerful. The design honours femininity without cliché, using a muted plum-and-gold palette that feels contemporary yet timeless. The emphasis is on the personal: these were real women with real stories.

Reminds people of: StoryCorps, the Obama Presidential Library website, the New Yorker profiles section, or high-quality podcast landing pages.

Best for audiences who connect through personal narrative and emotion. Ideal if the oral histories will be a centrepiece of the site, as the entire aesthetic is built around the idea of listening to and amplifying women's voices.
