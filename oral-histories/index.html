Oral Histories

First-person accounts from women who challenged discrimination

About the Oral Histories

The lawsuits of the 1970s were driven by courageous women who risked their careers and reputations to challenge discrimination. Their stories, preserved in oral histories and memoirs, provide invaluable insight into both the personal and collective dimensions of the movement.

This collection draws from multiple oral history projects and archives, providing access to first-person accounts, annotated transcripts, and contextual materials.

Notable Participants

Lynn Povich (Newsweek)

Lead plaintiff and author of "The Good Girls Revolt," Povich chronicled the organizing efforts and aftermath of the Newsweek suit. Her work provides crucial documentation of coalition-building strategies and the personal toll of litigation.

Betsy Wade (New York Times)

The first woman copy editor at the Times and lead plaintiff in Boylan et al. v. The New York Times, Wade's oral history and memoirs document both the struggle and the incremental victories. Available through the Herstory JAWS Oral History Project.

Elaine Auerbach (Reader's Digest)

Auerbach's leadership in organizing the Digest suit and her subsequent memoir, "Dirty Linen," highlight the challenges of coalition-building and the emotional toll of litigation.

Peggy Simpson, Virginia Sherlock, Shirley Christian (Associated Press)

These women played key roles in the AP suit and have contributed oral histories to various archives, providing perspectives on organizing in a wire service environment.

Mary Lou Butcher (Detroit News)

Oral history available through the Washington Press Club Foundation Oral History Project, housed at Columbia University and the National Press Club Library.

Eileen Shanahan (New York Times)

Oral history available through the Washington Press Club Foundation Oral History Project.

Major Oral History Collections

Herstory: JAWS Oral History Project

Thirty-three interviews with women journalists, including Betsy Wade, available online. These interviews provide first-person accounts of newsroom culture and legal battles.

Visit JAWS Herstory Project

Washington Press Club Foundation Oral History Project

Housed at Columbia University and the National Press Club Library, this collection includes interviews with participants in multiple discrimination suits.

Pennsylvania Newspaper Journalists Oral History Program

Program at Penn State documenting the experiences of journalists across the state, including those involved in discrimination suits.

Voices of Feminism Oral History Project (Smith College)

Collection includes interviews with labor, media, and grassroots activists, offering broader context for the women's movement.
