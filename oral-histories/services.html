Oral History Services

Preserving Stories, Experiences, and Institutional Memory

Every person, organization, and community has a story worth preserving. Through professional oral history interviewing and research, I help organizations document firsthand accounts, personal experiences, and historical perspectives that might otherwise be lost.

I am an oral historian, historian, author, and professor with extensive experience conducting and managing oral history projects. I hold a Ph.D. from Pennsylvania State University and am currently a Professor at Saint Louis University. My professional background combines academic historical research, journalism, and public history, allowing me to create oral histories that are both engaging and historically rigorous.

In addition to conducting oral history research for my own scholarly work, I have completed oral history projects for organizations including the Washington Press Club Foundation and the Newspaper Journalists Oral History Program. My experience interviewing individuals from diverse professional and personal backgrounds enables me to capture stories with accuracy, depth, and sensitivity.

A Historian's Approach to Oral History

Oral history is more than recording memories. It is a careful process of research, relationship building, interviewing, documentation, and preservation. My goal is to ensure that each interview captures not only facts and events, but also the personal experiences, reflections, and context that bring history to life.

My expertise is informed by years of historical research and writing. I am the author of:

  • Settling Scores: Women Who Sued the Media
  • How America Gets the News

My work examines media history, journalism, and the individuals who shape public discourse. Prior to my academic career, I worked in the Pittsburgh media industry, giving me firsthand experience in interviewing, storytelling, and documenting lived experiences.
