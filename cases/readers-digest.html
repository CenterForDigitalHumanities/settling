Reader's Digest (1972)

A landmark case that revealed extreme job segregation

← Back to Cases

Case Overview

Filed: 1972 | Settlement: 1977 | Lead Plaintiff: Elaine Auerbach

Legal Counsel: Harriet Rabb, Columbia Law School's Employment Rights Project

Elaine Auerbach and seven other women, represented by Harriet Rabb and Columbia Law School's Employment Rights Project, filed suit after discovering that women were systematically excluded from senior editorial positions at Reader's Digest. The case revealed shocking levels of job segregation and became a model for consent decree negotiations.

The Discrimination

The case revealed extreme patterns of sex-based job segregation:

  • 235 of 316 job titles were sex-segregated
  • Women were concentrated in low-paying fulfillment roles
  • Women were systematically excluded from senior editorial positions
  • Significant pay disparities for substantially equal work
  • Promotional pathways effectively closed to women

Key Plaintiffs

Elaine Auerbach

Auerbach's leadership in organizing the Reader's Digest suit and her subsequent memoir, "Dirty Linen," highlight the challenges of coalition-building and the emotional toll of litigation. Her decision to share the settlement broadly demonstrated solidarity with all women at the company, not just those who were named plaintiffs.

Legal Representation

Harriet S. Rabb and Howard Rubin

Rabb, director of Columbia Law School's Employment Rights Project, worked with Howard Rubin, a student and later instructor at the clinic, to build a comprehensive case that documented systemic discrimination across job categories. Their clinical approach combined legal strategy with grassroots organizing.

Outcome and Settlement

The 1977 settlement was one of the most substantial of the era:

  • $1.5 million in back pay
  • Requirement to implement diversity initiatives
  • Affirmative action plan with hiring and promotion goals
  • Regular monitoring and reporting to track progress

Notably, the plaintiffs chose to share the settlement with all women at the company, including those who had opted out of the suit. This decision reflected a commitment to collective justice rather than individual gain.

Impact and Legacy

The Reader's Digest case demonstrated the value of comprehensive documentation and coalition-building. By revealing the extent of job segregation, the plaintiffs and their attorneys made it impossible for the company to deny systemic discrimination.

The case influenced:

  • Subsequent litigation strategies at other organizations
  • EEOC guidelines on sex discrimination
  • Industry practices regarding job classification and promotion
  • The development of consent decree models that balanced enforcement with cooperation
