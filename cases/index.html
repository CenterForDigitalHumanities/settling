Landmark Cases

Major class-action lawsuits filed against news organizations in the 1970s

Overview

Between 1970 and the early 1980s, women at nearly every major print news organization in the United States filed class-action sex discrimination lawsuits. These cases exposed systemic patterns of gender discrimination and forced industry-wide reforms in hiring, promotion, and pay practices.

The following table summarizes the major cases filed during this period:

Legal Framework

These cases were made possible by landmark Supreme Court decisions that clarified and expanded the reach of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act:

  • Phillips v. Martin Marietta Corp. (1971): Established the "sex-plus" theory of discrimination
  • Griggs v. Duke Power Co. (1971): Ruled that employment practices with disparate impact must be justified by business necessity
  • Corning Glass Works v. Brennan (1974): Clarified proof requirements in pay discrimination cases
  • International Brotherhood of Teamsters v. United States (1977): Established presumption of relief for all class members in systemic discrimination cases

Impact and Legacy

The settlements in these cases included consent decrees mandating hiring and promotion goals, affirmative action plans, pay equity measures, and back pay. While wage gaps persisted, the cases forced news organizations to confront deeply embedded practices of gender discrimination and contributed to significant shifts in newsroom culture and editorial content.

Ironically, many of these news organizations failed to report on their own legal troubles, contributing to the relative obscurity of these landmark cases in public memory despite their profound impact on the industry.
