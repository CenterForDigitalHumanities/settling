Timeline

Key events in the fight against workplace discrimination

Historical Timeline

Date Event
1964Title VII of the Civil Rights Act prohibits workplace discrimination.
March 1970Women at Newsweek hold a press conference announcing an EEOC complaint.
May 1970Women at four Time Inc. magazines—Time, Life, Fortune, and Sports Illustrated—charge the company for sex discrimination.
August 1970The 50th anniversary of the Nineteenth Amendment, which granted women suffrage.
1971Columbia Law School establishes the Employment Rights Project.
1971The Time Inc. lawsuit settles out of court.
June 1972The Reader’s Digest women’s coalition file a formal EEOC complaint.
1972The Washington Post’s first sex discrimination lawsuit begins.
March 1972A group of Black Washington Post reporters, dubbed the Metro 7, file a race discrimination complaint with the EEOC.
1973Women at the New York Times file an EEOC complaint.
1973The Newsweek lawsuit settles out of court.
1973Lawyer Janice Goodman opens the first all-woman law firm in Manhattan.
October 1973Women at the AP file an EEOC complaint charging the wire service with discriminating against women and minorities.
October 1974Women working for the Register Publishing Co. in New Haven, Connecticut file an EEOC complaint.
1974Black reporters sue the New York Times for race discrimination. The suit settles in 1979.
1975The term “sexual harassment” is coined.
December 1976Women at the Detroit News hold a news conference announcing a lawsuit.
November 1977The Reader’s Digest lawsuit settles out of court with a $1.5 million settlement.
October 1978A New York Times settlement is reached.
November 1980A Washington Post settlement is reached.
1981A second suit begins at the Washington Post. The broad pay discrimination lawsuit includes women and people of color.
June 1982The Register Publishing Company goes to trial.
June 1983The AP lawsuit ends with an out-of-court $1.5 million settlement.
November 1983The Detroit News lawsuit settles out of court with a $300,000 settlement.
Spring 2003The second Washington Post lawsuit settles.
Spring 2021The Washington Post announces Sally Buzbee as its first female executive editor.
